Splat2Act: From Captured Scenes to Personalized VLA Manipulation
Source: ARXIV
Authors: Xiaomeng Ye, Krishna Jaganathan, Gyubeum Lim, Gunjan Chhablani, Muhammad Zubair Irshad, Zsolt Kira

Abstract
A user who brings a generalist manipulation policy into their home or office wants it to pick up their objects from their shelves, counters, and tables. Yet even a strong generalist like $π_{0.5}$ achieves 0% pickup success in our captured deployment scenes without adaptation, and collecting real demonstrations in every user's environment is prohibitively expensive. We present Splat2Act, an automated real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that personalizes a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policy to a specific scene and object set using only a handheld phone scan and images of the user's objects. Unlike prior real-to-sim work that reconstructs individual tabletops, Splat2Act operates on building-scale indoor environments: it builds a photorealistic digital twin with 3D Gaussian Splatting and collision geometry, automatically discovers tabletop and non-tabletop receptacles, constructs feasible pickup tasks, and generates successful demonstrations with a privileged simulation oracle, with only scene and object capture required from a human. Applied to 101 scanned campus environments with a median navigable area of 104.87 m$^2$ and 7 receptacles, the pipeline yields 852 receptacles and over 68,000 pickup demonstrations. We fine-tune $π_{0.5}$ in two stages: Stage 1 adapts to hundreds of public object assets, drawn either from the deployment scene alone or from all 101 scenes; Stage 2 personalizes to the user's own objects on the deployment scene's receptacles. Our strongest personalized policy reaches 62.5% aggregate pickup success in the target scene. Deployed on a real Stretch 3, the same policy achieves 63.9% success on a 12-task subset, closely tracking its 66.7% simulated success on those tasks. These results suggest that automated real-to-sim-to-real training can personalize VLA manipulation policies using only simulation-oracle data.

Noise Space Learning for VLA Adaptation

Authors: Junjie Lu, Chuheng Zhang, Yucen Wang, Yuhua Jiang, Xinyao Qin, Min Xu, Li Zhao

Abstract
Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models encode broad manipulation priors, yet adapting them to specific tasks requires balancing limited robot interaction, computational resources, and safety constraints. Noise space learning (NSL) adapts flow-matching-based VLAs by learning a context-conditioned distribution over noise while keeping the pretrained VLA fixed. Building on prior noise-steering methods, we formulate NSL as an interface for VLA adaptation that accommodates different learning algorithms and instantiate it with PPO, SAC, and AWR. Theoretically, we provide an information-theoretic analysis of how NSL enables efficient adaptation. Empirically, we study these NSL algorithms on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and four real-robot manipulation tasks under interaction, computation, and execution constraints. Across these settings, we develop a suite of effective NSL variants and derive practical recommendations for choosing among them under different resource and deployment regimes.

Chronos Policy: Learning Robot Policies on Physical Time

Comments: Project website: https://changhaowang.github.io/chronospolicy/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robot policies often convert sensing and control into fixed-rate observation-action sequences, leaving physical time implicit in sequence position. This assumption breaks when observation rates or delays vary, or when inference latency changes. We introduce Chronos Policy, which makes physical time explicit by conditioning on observation capture times and desired action execution times. We study two ways to incorporate time into a diffusion transformer: adding timestamp embeddings to token features, or encoding physical time in positional encoding through time-aware rotary positional encodings (time-aware RoPE). We find that time-aware RoPE is more robust, allowing attention to depend directly on physical time while keeping token content unchanged. During training, we temporally randomize which observations are sampled and when the action chunk begins, exposing the policy to a wider range of sensing and execution schedules that may occur during deployments. This also enables learning from asynchronous, multi-rate data without forcing all measurements onto a fixed temporal grid. Across simulation and real-world experiments, Chronos Policy improves performance on dynamic tasks, where observation-to-action timing is critical, and on multisensory tasks, where the relative timing between observations matters. On standard quasi-static tasks, it preserves nominal performance while improving robustness to changes in sensing and inference timing.

Abstract
Deep-space communications operate under intermittent connectivity, long propagation delays, and relay storage constraints. In such networks, the Bundle Protocol enables store-carry-forward communication, requiring relay nodes to manage limited storage and scarce contact opportunities. Relay-side scheduling and dropping decisions are inherently goal-oriented: transmission resources should be allocated to bundles most valuable to downstream mission objectives. Conventional schedulers, such as FIFO, do not explicitly optimize task utility and may provide unfair service across heterogeneous planetary sources. This paper presents a closed-loop modeling and learning framework for relay-side bundle and flow management in a contact-driven deep-space network, where lunar and Martian updates are forwarded through a shared-buffer relay to Earth. We quantify destination-side information freshness using excess age $Δ_i^e(t)=[Δ_i(t)-d_i(t)]^+$, where $Δ_i(t)$ denotes the Age of Information of source $i$ and $d_i(t)$ is a source-dependent delay baseline. Based on this metric, we define a goal-oriented loss that supports source-dependent nonlinear penalties, allowing prioritization according to mission relevance. We formulate relay control as a constrained semi-Markov decision process (CSMDP) and solve it using masked Lagrangian Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Feasibility-aware action masking restricts transmissions to bundles that can complete within the remaining contact window. DTNSim simulations indicate improved age-based utility and inter-flow fairness compared with baseline schedulers. The proposed framework jointly optimizes relay-side bundle scheduling, dropping, and flow control under contact-driven dynamics with explicit fairness considerations across heterogeneous sources.

Abstract
Renewable power sources have low production costs, but may result in high balancing costs due to the inherent production uncertainty. Current day-ahead markets elicit only point production profiles and neglect the degree of uncertainty associated with each generating asset, preventing the market operator from accounting for balancing costs in day-ahead dispatch and ancillary service procurement. An electricity market case study validates the theoretical claims, illustrating the effectiveness of the proposed mechanism in reducing system cost.

Abstract
Target tracking over wide areas using a network of sensor agents is a challenging problem when agents have limited sensing coverage and communication topologies vary over time. Existing distributed filtering approaches commonly assume that each agent, or at least one of its neighbors, can directly observe the target, and further rely on fixed network topologies, global network parameters, or multiple consensus rounds per time step. These requirements limit their applicability in wide-area network deployments. In this paper, we propose an Information-Weighted Distributed Kalman Filter (IWDKF) that relaxes these assumptions through a weighted-consensus mechanism applicable to arbitrary switching topologies satisfying joint connectivity. The proposed approach requires no prior knowledge of global network properties and limits communication overhead to a single consensus exchange per time step, while enabling information fusion even when some agents lack direct target measurements. The weighted-consensus covariance matrix is shown to remain positive definite, and the resulting consensus update is proven to maintain estimate consistency. It is also established that the proposed weighting strategy preserves the asymptotic agreement property of the underlying consensus protocol under joint connectivity. Numerical simulations of a multi-target tracking scenario involving a network of sensor agents demonstrate the improved estimation accuracy of the proposed IWDKF under both fixed and switching network topologies. The performance of the proposed filter is close to that of the centralized Kalman filter, and is compared with three existing distributed filters.

Abstract
Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) is critical for autonomous perception, yet its performance is known to degrade in adverse weather. While existing literature models snow as atmospheric noise that degrades performance, our granular evaluation on the WADS-3D and CADC datasets reveals a counter-intuitive phenomenon: detection accuracy consistently improves as snow intensity increases. Through ablation study and meta-information analysis, we reconcile this by confirming that while falling snow acts as noise, its negative impact is outweighed by a dominant, concurrent factor: significantly increased object point count. We hypothesize that this object point count boost acts as a geometric enhancement, driven by snow accumulation increasing vehicle surface area and a shift from specular to diffuse reflectance. Our findings demonstrate that evaluating perception systems using broad weather-severity bins without accounting for correlated object-level data can lead to misleading conclusions about detector robustness. We conclude that accurate evaluation requires decoupling atmospheric noise from surface accumulation effects and outline future research to validate these physical models. Abstract
We present HorizonNav, a video diffusion-based framework for anticipatory robot navigation that transforms a single RGB image and a text prompt into a geometrically aligned representation of plausible surrounding 3D space. Given the initial observation and a textual description of the reconstruction objective, a vision-language model, guided by designed instructions, interprets the scene and constructs structured prompts for a video diffusion model. The resulting video sequence provides plausible future observations of the environment, which are subsequently processed using monocular ORB-SLAM3 for camera pose estimation and Video Depth Anything for dense depth prediction. Our central contribution is a geometry-guided depth alignment stage that combines the estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to constrain dense depth predictions across multiple views. This alignment improves the geometric consistency of the reconstructed 3D environment, addressing inconsistencies that temporal coherence of the generated video alone cannot resolve. The experimental evaluation of navigation in imagined 3D environment shows that across a 19-scene horizon study, planning-feasible scenes increase from 13/19 at P20 to 15/19 at P81, while successful RRT trials increase from 62/90 to 75/90. In a separate corridor study, 6 of 25 evaluable generated-camera trajectories violate the reconstructed collision constraints; RRT planner recovers feasible alternatives in 5 of those 6 cases. A larger archive contains 58 usable reconstructions from 67 attempted cases, of which 36 support a downstream plan and 22 do not, exposing reconstruction and free-space failure modes rather than hiding them without lidar. Across 15 corridor reconstructions, the mean reconstruction error is approximately 0.30 m at matched structural landmarks.

Abstract
Safety-critical control of uncertain, adaptive systems often relies on conservative, worst-case uncertainty bounds that limit closed-loop performance. Online conformal prediction is a powerful data-driven method for quantifying uncertainty when truth values of predicted outputs are revealed online; however, for systems that adapt the dynamics without measurements of the state derivatives, standard online conformal prediction is insufficient to quantify the model uncertainty. We propose Staggered Integral Online Conformal Prediction (SI-OCP), an algorithm utilizing an integral score function to quantify the lumped effect of disturbance and learning error. This approach provides long-run coverage guarantees, resulting in long-run safety when synthesized with safety-critical controllers, including robust tube model predictive control. Finally, we validate the proposed approach through a numerical simulation of an all-layer deep neural network (DNN) adaptive quadcopter using robust tube (MPC), highlighting the applicability of our method to complex learning parameterizations and control strategies.

Abstract
Nearly every autonomous or semi-autonomous flipper controller assumes access to an elevation map of the terrain under and in front of the robot. The terrain beneath the chassis is occluded in the sensors, so the map is remembered through odometry integration, whose drift corrupts it. We present Stabilizing Anti-Stuck (SAS), a mapless controller that combines a stabilization term for body roll and pitch with an anti-stuck term that frees the robot when it gets stuck. As input, SAS uses the IMU, the flipper joint angles, and a motion cue that only tells whether the robot is moving. The cue is computed over a short window and never accumulated, so SAS does not inherit the odometry-integration drift that corrupts the map. We deploy SAS on a tracked robot with four flippers and evaluate it against manual teleoperation and prior flipper controllers in a large comparison, both in simulation and on a real obstacle. In simulation and on the real robot, SAS achieves traversal quality comparable to state-of-the-art map-based controllers. On a log hidden in tall grass, invisible to the LiDAR, the best map-based controller gets stuck while SAS traverses the hidden obstacle without a problem.

Abstract
Robots deployed in realistic settings will accumulate experience across many sessions and tasks over their deployment. The robot's tasks may often require it to remember information from multiple sessions ago, making long-context robot memory important for real-world deployments. However, most robot-memory benchmarks today are based on single episodes or a short context. To measure how current robot memory systems perform on longer sessions with more distractions, we introduce RoboMME-Interference, a cross-session benchmark built on RoboMME (Dai et al., 2026). For each query episode, we construct a session history using the query's relevant prior demonstration followed by a controlled number of unrelated sessions, which we provide to the VLA as memory and measure accuracy. Running RoboMME's released memory-augmented $π_{0.5}$ variants unmodified through this benchmark, we find that while perceptual memory variants improve success when given the history without any distractors, they decay strongly and steadily as unrelated sessions accumulate. The subgoal variants, which read the history with a vision-language model and pass written subgoals to the policy, improve less at their best but hold more of that improvement as distractors accumulate. Adding a retrieval step to the strongest perceptual variant, which selects the section of history most visually similar to the robot's current view and passes only that section to the policy, restores its no-distractor success rate at every interference level. With this release, we emphasize the importance of long-context memory and robustness to interference and show that current systems largely fail on such capabilities. The project page, videos, code, and data are at https://robotmemorybench.com.

Abstract
A central challenge in embodied recursive self-improvement is enabling agents to convert interaction history into capabilities that persist beyond one world and transfer to later tasks. A saved memory or successful final action does not by itself establish that a reusable capability was acquired; evaluation must separate capability change from carried-over resources, unequal source exposure, and early execution failures. We construct MC-EvoBench as a controlled Minecraft benchmark with a fixed catalog of 19 registered evolution chains, including 12 primary evaluation chains and seven calibration or probe chains, together with three graph-aligned paired probe families for transfer. The benchmark represents each task as a task-state graph, defines stage-level environment verifiers, assigns matched source and target roles, resets the target world, and permits only a declared agent artifact to cross the source-target boundary. For each probe, no-prior, related, unrelated, and shuffled source conditions are evaluated under matched environment-step budgets and five seeds; we report TSR, WGP, and VMP and retain provenance records. The benchmark reveals a consistent direct-capability gap between controller and planner-backed variants, while related experience yields no stable transfer: it does not improve either tool probe, its small wood-species gain hinges on one seed, and all intervals include zero. Stage audits further show that early wood-collection and crafting bottlenecks often leave later capabilities unobserved. MC-EvoBench thus offers a controlled testbed for durable, transferable embodied capabilities. Code and data are available at https://github.com/xzw-ustc/mc-bench-open-source

Abstract
Actuator amplitude and rate saturation (A\&RSat), together with their consequent windup problem, have long been recognised as challenges in control systems. Anti-windup (AW) solutions have been developed over the past decades, which can generally be categorised into two main groups: classical and modern anti-windup (CAW and MAW) approaches. Classical methods have provided simple and effective results, mainly addressing amplitude saturation. In contrast, modern approaches offer powerful and theoretically sound solutions capable of handling both amplitude and rate saturations. However, MAW's derivation process often imposes restrictive conditions and can be complex to apply in practical engineering problems. Nevertheless, the literature has paid limited attention (if not entirely ignored) to the potential of simple yet effective CAW schemes that can operate in the presence of both A\&RSat elements. This paper revisits this issue and proposes modifications to two well-known controllers: PID and LQI. The obtained results, benchmarked on the REMUS AUV yaw control problem and compared with constrained MPC, indicate that these classical techniques can still provide simple yet effective solutions with comparable performance, at least for SISO systems. These findings may stimulate further research into solutions that achieve comparable performance with only one (or a limited number of) additional tuning parameters and straightforward implementation.

Abstract
Scaling generalist robotic policies is limited by the high cost of collecting embodied interaction data. Large-scale egocentric human videos offer a rich alternative, but they lack robot action labels, and existing action annotation methods are often noisy under real-world visual complexity and diverse manipulation dynamics. We introduce LAPT-Scale, a scalable latent action pre-training paradigm with two components: LAPT-Scale-LAM learns continuous latent actions by explaining frame-to-frame visual transitions in human videos and uses them as human-derived supervision, while LAPT-Scale-Policy trains a VLA policy through human-video latent-action pre-training, target-robot embodiment alignment, and task-specific post-training. Unlike prior vector-quantized LAMs, LAPT-Scale-LAM uses a continuous latent space to preserve fine-grained motion and interaction dynamics. We further combine perceptual, semantic, and optical-flow alignment signals to improve transition-level supervision beyond pixel reconstruction. Trained on 23.3K hours of egocentric video, LAPT-Scale-LAM provides implicit action supervision for policy pre-training, yielding consistent scaling gains and improving real-robot success from 63.3% to 85.0% as annotated human video scales from 1K to 5K hours. The learned policy also generalizes to unseen visual conditions.

Abstract
For fully actuated rigid robots, kinematic inversion is a purely geometric problem, efficiently solved by closed-loop inverse kinematics (CLIK) schemes that compute joint velocities to position the robot body in space. For underactuated soft robots, however, not all configurations are attainable through control action, making kinematic inversion extremely ill-posed. Extensions of CLIK address this by introducing end-to-end mappings from actuation to task space for the controller to operate on, but typically assume finite dimensions of the underlying virtual configuration space. In this work, we formulate CLIK in the infinite-dimensional domain to reason about the entire soft robot shape while solving tasks, thereby skipping intermediate approximation steps. We do this by composing an actuation-to-shape map with a shape-to-task map, deriving the differential end-to-end kinematics via an infinite-dimensional chain rule, and thereby obtaining a Jacobian-based CLIK algorithm. Since complex soft-robot models are rarely available in closed form, and neither the actuation-to-shape mapping nor its Jacobian can typically be obtained analytically, we propose to learn it using differentiable neural operator networks. We first present an analytical study on a constant-curvature segment, and then apply the neural version of the algorithm to a three-fiber soft robotic arm whose underlying model relies on morphoelasticity and active filament theory.

Abstract
Safe exploration remains a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), limiting the deployment of RL agents in the real world. We propose Sampling-Based Safe Reinforcement Learning (SBSRL), a model-based RL algorithm that maintains safety throughout the learning process by enforcing constraints jointly across a finite set of dynamics samples. This formulation approximates an intractable worst-case optimization over uncertain dynamics and enables practical safety guarantees in continuous domains. We further introduce an exploration strategy based on constraining epistemic uncertainty, eliminating the need for explicit exploration bonuses. Under regularity conditions, we derive high-probability guarantees of safety throughout learning and a finite-time sample complexity bound for recovering a near-optimal policy. Empirically, SBSRL achieves safe and efficient exploration both in simulation and in real robotic hardware, and readily extends to practical deep-ensemble implementations that scale to high-dimensional continuous control problems.

Abstract
Harvesting technologies have evolved from conventional mechanized systems based on bulk mechanical interaction toward increasingly automated and robotic systems capable of perception, decision-making, and localized actuation. This evolution has also created a classification problem, as harvesting systems are commonly described according to crop type, mechanical removal principle, sensing technology, robotic architecture, or degree of automation, making direct comparison difficult. This paper proposes a unified taxonomy for agricultural harvesting technologies based on two primary classification axes: the physical principle governing plant-machine interaction and the level of automation incorporated into the harvesting process. Selectivity is treated as an emergent functional attribute that expresses the biological and spatial scale at which perception and decision-making influence physical actuation. Conventional mechanized methods, including cutting, pulling, vibration, impact, brushing, pneumatic action, and combined systems, are analyzed together with semi-automated and robotic harvesting approaches. The analysis shows that increasing automation does not necessarily result in greater selectivity. A qualitative transition occurs when perception and decision-making directly influence product-level actuation, shifting harvesting from distributed or bulk interaction toward localized and selective manipulation. The proposed framework provides a common conceptual basis for positioning and comparing mechanized, automated, and robotic harvesting technologies. By separating the physical mechanism of product removal from the automation level and resulting selectivity, the taxonomy clarifies technological boundaries, identifies intermediate configurations, and supports the analysis and development of future harvesting systems.

Abstract
Accurate monitoring of power flow is fundamental to the secure and reliable operation of modern power systems. Existing data-driven power flow estimation methods typically assume that synchronized measurements are available at every bus, requiring extensive deployment of phasor measurement units (PMUs). This paper proposes a learning-based framework that minimizes the number of installed PMUs while maintaining high-accuracy systemwide power flow estimation. Abstract
This paper presents a dual-quadrotor hook- suspended transport system for cooperative aerial manipulation of a payload that is not rigidly attached to a fixed point along the cable. Two quadrotors are connected by a single inextensible cable that passes through a frictionless hook mounted on the payload, allowing the cable to slide between its two segments. This introduces a cable-distribution variable that couples the quadrotor motion, cable geometry, and payload dynamics. A feasible reference-generation framework is formulated using the desired payload pose, cable distribution, and system shape while satisfying the cable-length constraint and elliptical workspace. The quadrotors track the geometrically generated trajectories while maintaining positive cable tension and respecting actuator limits. The payload pose is estimated from the system geometry, and its measured position is used only to validate the desired trajectory. The approach is evaluated through experimental demonstrations.

Abstract

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies issue sparse targets whose arrival at the robot controller is delayed and irregular, so a reasonable predicted action can still produce abrupt motion near people. Remedies are evaluated on different streams, delays and metrics. JitterVLA replays identical commands and arrival traces through alternative controllers, isolating the controller's effect. We apply it to synthetic reaches, hand trajectories from 20 people, three open VLA pipelines (838 episodes) and a physical UR3. Interpolation lowered modeled p99 jerk for every source, by 83% (615 to 106 m/s^3) on human motion with 200 ms delay, at a 2.0 cm tracking cost, and the effect replicated on 598 new episodes; jerk-limited generation lowered jerk further at a larger cost. Entropy-discontinuity associations had opposite, replicated signs in two pipelines, and entropy gating helped in one but not the other. On the UR3, interpolation lowered accelerometer-derived jerk by 3.0-14.0% in all six exploratory pairs, while a position-derived metric did not resolve the contrast. JitterVLA supports screening before human-centred studies; code and data will be released.

Abstract
Numerous approaches have been proposed for multi-AUV path planning in oceanic environments, with many leveraging deep reinforcement learning (DRL). However, existing methods often fail to account for the physical constraints of actual AUV movement, and the assumed environments lack realism, thereby limiting their practical application. Abstract
To address the limited endurance of Unmanned Surface Vehicles (USVs) in time-varying sea conditions, this study investigates global energy-efficient path planning that balances obstacle avoidance and energy efficiency. First, based on ship seakeeping theory, an energy consumption model incorporating wave height, wave period, speed, and wave-encounter angle is constructed to represent the impact of dynamic sea states on resistance. Second, the planning problem is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). Built upon the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, a Bimodal Spatio-Temporal Feature Fusion Network (BSTFN) is proposed to achieve deep fusion of spatial perception information and historical temporal sea state sequences for decision-making. Furthermore, a composite reward function is designed, integrating energy consumption penalties, heading guidance, and smoothness constraints.
